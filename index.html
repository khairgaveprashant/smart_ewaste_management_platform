The Challenge

Small Devices. Big Responsibility.

Old electronics can contain valuable materials, but improper disposal can also create environmental and safety risks.

Unused Electronics

Old phones, chargers, laptops, and accessories often remain unused because people do not know where to take them.

Incorrect Disposal

Improper handling can expose people and the environment to hazardous substances such as lead, mercury, and toxic flame retardants.

Recoverable Resources

Responsible reuse, repair, and recycling can extend product life and help recover useful materials such as copper, aluminum, and precious metals.

System Architecture Comparison

Linear vs. Circular Approaches

Traditional Linear Model

Take → Make → Dispose

Manufacture → Use → Dispose

Electronic devices are manufactured from finite raw minerals, sold, used for a brief period, and dumped into landfill sites or incinerators, discarding valuable recoverable metals and risking toxic heavy-metal contamination.

E-Cycle Circular Model

Recover → Restore → Regenerate

Manufacture → Use → Reuse/Repair → Further Use → Appropriate Recycling → Material Recovery

Recycling is essential, but retaining useful devices through reuse and repair prevents unnecessary waste more effectively. Materials that reach end-of-life undergo authorized recovery to feed back into manufacturing loops.

How Smart Segregation Works

The current prototype uses rule-based classification. It evaluates the registered category and reported condition to recommend a potential next step. Safety-sensitive items receive special handling guidance, and uncertain records can be flagged for manual review.

Note: Preliminary recommendation generated by rule-based segregation. Does not replace professional inspection or instructions from an authorized recycler. Not an AI or machine-learning model.

How Is the Estimated Value Calculated?

Formula: Estimated Gross Recovery Value = Σ (Recorded Total Weight in kg × Illustrative Rate in ₹/kg).

Worked Example: A laptop weighing 2.00 kg at an illustrative rate of ₹80/kg generates an estimated value of 2.00 × 80 = ₹160.00. This represents theoretical gross material recovery potential and does not account for logistics, sorting costs, depollution, or guaranteed recycler payout.
